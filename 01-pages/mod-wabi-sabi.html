wabi-sabi and my healing work

Wabi-sabi is philosophically and aesthetically integral to everything I believe about the most beautiful ways to live life. Its understandings of impermanence, imperfection, simplicity, and the power even within attrition (especially within attrition as I and my clients age) are integral to my sense of healing and spiritual practice, and to the practice of well-being at every level.

Wabi-sabi will be a guiding force in the spiritual and philosophical understanding of bridging the gap between man’s way and the world that God made, to underscore everything that I do.

It is the celebration of the fullness and balance of life that is the underlying purpose of a home, a healing retreat, or community. “Combine the skills of architecture, interior and landscape design, art, food preparation and service that coalesce into a quietly exciting event that thematically coheres.”

  • Learn to see: “Slow way down, be patient and look very closely.”
  • Assist our co-creator to achieve an environment for the experience of beauty. “Wabi-sabi suggests that beauty is a dynamic event that occurs between you and something else. Beauty can spontaneously occur at any moment given the proper circumstances, context, or point of view. Beauty is thus an altered state of consciousness, an extraordinary moment of poetry and grace.”
  • Assist our co-creators in placing our materialism in perspective. “Even at the most austere level of material existence, we still live in a world of things. Wabi-sabi is exactly about the delicate balance between the pleasure we get from things and the pleasure we get from the freedom from things.”
  • Celebrate naturalness. “Things wabi-sabi are expressions of time frozen. They are made of materials that are visibly vulnerable to the effects of weathering and human treatment. They record the sun, wind, rain, heat, and cold in a language of discoloration, rust, tarnish, stain, warping, shrinking, shriveling, and cracking. Their nicks, chips, bruises, scars, dents, peeling, and other forms of attrition are a testament to histories of use and misuse. Though things wabi-sabi may be at the point of dematerialization (or materialization), extremely faint, fragile or desiccated, they still possess an undiminished poise and strength of character.”
  • “Things wabi-sabi are unstudied and inevitable looking. They do not blare out ‘I am important’ or demand to be the center of attention. They are understated and unassuming, yet not without presence or quiet authority. Things wabi-sabi easily coexist with the rest of their environment.”
  • “Simplicity is at the core of things wabi-sabi. The simplicity of wabi-sabi is probably best described as the state of grace arrived at by a sober, modest, heartfelt intelligence. The main strategy of this intelligence is economy of means. Pare down to the essence, but don’t remove the poetry. Keep things clean and unencumbered, but don’t sterilize.”

Within my spiritual and healing practice, wabi-sabi is at the heart of all aesthetics involved in the experience of all five senses: sight (the environment, implements I use, the clothes I wear, the ikebana floral arrangements, the sheets, towels, robes), sound, smell, touch, and taste.

Create an environment where one “can pay maximum attention to everything happening at this very moment” — an environment in which “to be here now.”
