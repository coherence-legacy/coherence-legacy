What is viGoArtes?

viGo in a reduction of Viking Gaucho

I am a guest, a playful thief of culture, claiming my artist’s “nom de plume”, vikinGaucho—a fusion of gaucho’s earthy grit and my Viking blood, stirred by my ancestral Genghis Khan’s wild echo. Having come from the Cowboy dust of America’s west, I find kinship in the “patria” of Uruguay’s still thriving ranching traditions.

Uruguay, our generous muse, has sparked a second life of art, revealing inspired divine natural beauty at every turn, in the most intimate experiences of the immediate nature in which we live to the vistas vast and skies eternal.

At seventy, I dance as an eccentric, free to conjure joy, to weave mischief and wonder into whatever moves my heart’s desire.

Uruguay, a Quiet Embrace

viGoArtes is my tender homage to Uruguay, where earth and sky, flora and fauna entwine in pristine natural simplicity. The “campo” in our new home country unfurls in gentle waves, alive with flora’s infinite variations, and fauna’s abundant and still thriving wild freedom.

In Uruguay, simplicity is a sacred art: a sunset painting the heavens, a parrilla’s smoky welcome, the laughter of kin and neighbors weaving life’s daily tapestry. Uruguay, with its languid country roads and charmingly tangled ways, is no land of flaws but a canvas of quirks, each a stroke of grace.

This is the echo of Tao Te Ching’s dream—a small land, souls content to savor home, where weapons go unused, tools rest often, and hearts linger in the moment’s glow. Uruguay breathes this tranquility, its unhurried pulse a gift, an invitation to dance with time’s gentle rhythm. We hold this land in our hearts, grateful for its embrace, with its wide open deep blue sky, a mirror to eternity.

Las Lunas de San Francisco

Las Lunas de San Francisco: A Journey to Peace in Uruguay

In November 2015, we left behind the jagged peaks of Colorado’s Rockies and California’s Sierras, trading their wild beauty for the gentle embrace of Uruguay. Our former homes, though breathtaking, were defined by howling winters, untamed wildlife, and the creeping pressures of modern life. Seeking sovereign simplicity, we crossed hemispheres to find tranquility in Uruguay’s rolling countryside.

Over the past decade, we’ve called several corners of this serene nation home—from windswept beachfront homes to secluded chacras nestled among eucalyptus groves. Each place taught us to savor Uruguay’s unhurried rhythms. Yet, it was Las Lunas de San Francisco, our leased ancient stone house in the campo, that truly captured our hearts.

Las Lunas is more than a home; it’s a sanctuary. Its weathered stone walls, warmed by the sun, seem to hum with history. Each morning, we sip mate in the salon or out on the courtyard, watching mist rise from the fields as birdsong fills the air, sheep cows, and horses grazing. These moments fuel what we call our “Life’s Second Adventure”—a deliberate shift to a slower, more creative existence in our later years. Here, inspiration flows freely: a painting born from the golden hues of a sunset, a poem sparked by the rustle of leaves.

This rare treasure of a place has gifted us infinite gratitude. Las Lunas de San Francisco is not just where we live—it’s where we’ve come alive.

ikebana-Vida / ikeVida

The Japanese art of Ikebana, the art of shaping branches, rocks, moss, stems and blooms into beautiful “arrangements”, has been one of my long-practiced art-forms in my prior life in design and architecture.

I experienced its personal revival in Uruguay’s wild, thorny, rugged bounty. Gathering remarkable logs, sculptured branches, and intricate Ligustro twigs from our forest, I began to imagine beyond vases and table top expressions.

The whole of Lunas de San Francisco became not just our “arrangement”, it has become a “living ikebana world”, where house, courtyard, orchards, fields, forest and stream, wildlife, and livestock vibrate with all of earth’s intricate and beautiful creations.

Ikebana-Vida, ikeVida, is our name for this living ikebana life. It is a spontaneous reverence to creation’s genius. It is a way of life now for us, art-making woven from nature, from earth, fire, water and aether, the very breath of the land.

viGoArtes, for the love of wood

Independent of my life’s work in design and architecture, woodworking has always called to me. From my earliest furniture pieces, a chess table at twelve, and a curvaceous sculptural desk at nineteen years of age, and decades of building five of my own homes and ranches, furniture, and wooden boat construction and maintenance.

I had thought I was long finished with this mode of self-expression. But…Cypress, Casuarina, Black Acacia, Fresno, Ligustro, Lapacho proved too much to resist!! Stumps, planks, branches, raw and unpolished, right here at Lunas de San Francisco, or very nearby, inspired furniture, sculpture, and art expressions in communion with nature’s own infinite artistic expression of twisted branches, wild textures, unfathomable patterns.

Luces del Fuego/Firelights

From the campo’s heart, an unexpected muse emerged: our firewood! Each cracked, splintered piece, gathered daily to warm Lunas de San Francisco, hums with stories—etched by time, shaped by earth’s quiet alchemy. These fragments birthed Luces del Fuego, Firelights—my signature light sculptures where fire, earth, water, and aether dance in dynamic illuminated presence.

From small glowing bedside “sculpturitos”, to monumental beacons for grand public spaces are ablaze with nature’s untamed grace. Each a vibrant expression of Uruguay’s unique natural spirit. This call is to art-making, beyond craft, a living dialogue with the land’s infinite forms.

Bocetos de Uruguay

Bocetos de Uruguay, inspired by Miles Davis’ famous album, Sketches of Spain, is our love letter to a lifetime in design and architecture distilled into a singular expression —a book I am now writing and illustrating tracing Las Lunas de San Francisco’s transformation from a traditional Uruguayan country house into a living Ikebana world we live within. In this endeavor we are subtracting all that is not an element of earth, fire, water, and aether. And celebrating all that is the miraculous product of life’s expression of itself.

We are distilling the entire Casa de Campo into an exploration of the miraculous infinite beauty and creativity of nature herself. This very personal diary of the Lunas de San Francisco transformation in book form is our journey, shared in gratitude’s tender light.
