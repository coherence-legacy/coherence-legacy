permaculture principals used

  • observation: assessing what was available in way of materials, existing elements, sun patterns
  • resource: existing gardens/plants, soils, infrastructure
  • the problem is the solution: weeds—use for mulch/pest control/medicinal. fallen fruit feed sheep
  • biological resources: e.g. natural pest control (chickens, garlic, peppermint)
  • use onsite resources: soils, plants, building materials, well/spring, water storage tanks, existing irrigation
  • one calorie in/one calorie out: building soil
  • energy recycling: making the most use of water, solar energy, manure, etc. before they leave the system
  • every element supports many functions: edible fences; ponds catch rainfall, excess runoff from other catchment systems, growing pond plants that can be used to feed animals or create mulch, provide irrigation to gardens or orchards, creates microclimate, digging provides clay for building. chickens provide eggs, manure and pest control.
  • every function is supported by many elements: composting is supported by animals, plant and pond material. pond is supported by excess rainwater cachement system runoff, well water
  • relative location: located water tanks, compost piles, grey water systems, kitchen garden near locations would be used.
  • diversity: animals, fruit, veggies, plants for pollinators, ponds, rainwater cachement and ponds, swales
  • local focus: creating own currency, trading with neighbors
  • stocking: not over-stocking animals in terms of how much space/feed we have
  • stacking: vertical stacking of functional plants.
  • pond (see above), animals (goats, chickens provide food in milk, eggs, meat and also manure for composting and help shred materials for composting), buildings (green roofs, housing, rainwater cachement)
  • succession of evolution: first year, do sheet mulching and grow cover crops for future farming success.
  • edge: adding ponds, food forests, hedges, etc for maximum edge utilization
