viGoArtes

artist statement

I have always wanted a way to make poetry that is automatic, spontaneous, native, and real to me. I’ve tried on so many different forms of expressing essence in free and minimal terms but to no avail. Then Vakhs just happened into my field of consciousness this year and now I see them everywhere…..

I am not concerned with achievement –
only the freedom to love today what is at hand.
In this simplicity without motive or motif,
the now becomes eternal

vakhs

Now I write these Kashmiri “Vakhs” every day….actually I write them in my mind all the time…..Vakhs are a set of four verses, quatrains, that express the essence of life experience using simplicity, intensity and universality. They are deeply rooted in 14th-century Kashmiri Shaivism blending spiritual insight, self-realization, and devotion. For viGo, they are my way to express and live IN art.

I dissect the rose
petal by petal
to understand its fragrance.
God laughs aloud —
“Fool, it was never that simple.”
Look deeper still.

“Vakhs” have come to be an exquisite way of communicating what I am experiencing in everyday life, and in this artistic expression at this later stage in my - “Life’s Second Adventure”

Like the Vakhs, my art making in sculpture, painting, furniture, light forming shares these essential elements:

  • Spontaneity
  • Reverence for the natural
  • Being drawn into the essence of the materials, the “God Stuff” I’m working with
  • Working with what materials want to be, not what I want them to be (there’s a continuous give and take here)

fifty years

I spent my fifty-year career in design, architecture, permaculture, and estate planning- where years would pass between inspiration and realization……….and though I loved those years of creativity, this new and unexpected chapter in my creative life is all about naturalness, spontaneity, freedom, flow, joy and instant gratification.

As this new creative bug began to gnaw on me, I resolved that if I did take this plunge and pursue another stage of “art-making”, I would never “work a day again in my life”. If what was next, and next, and next was not pure joy, I would move on to something that was without strain.

I’m four years now into this wee-folly of creation, and nothing that is not deeply inspiring has yet to dissuade me. I’m am enthralled every day to be an active participant and co-creator with nature and the all-that-is. Instead of leading, I have learned to listen, to feel, to wait, to encourage more life, a second life, to emerge from the materials that surround us here at Las Lunas de San Francisco in Uruguay’s “High Country” and make their way into my atelier. Like the sculptor Rodin, I am more often than not, just subtracting all that it is not the experience withing wanting to come forward.

when I grow up…

There is something else……when I was 15 years-old I experienced a few days in Art Carpenters “Espenet” workshop in Bolinas California making snowshoes for my Outward Bound-like school I was in for a year. I love everything about the environment. I vowed that my career would be designing and making fine furniture and wood-art. I did a lot of it over the years, but the design part of that took hold very early on as I had the ability. But I would say, even as late as four years ago, that I would still like to be a fine furniture maker when I grow up……….Interesting that I am finally growing up and the fine wood working now also includes furniture and wood-arts, sculpture, illuminated art, painting….and “Valks”……
