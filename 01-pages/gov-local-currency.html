local currency

Here at Silent Fire Ranch we have a local currency system called the Yucca system. Almost everyone here has an YUCCA account, and we can buy almost anything we want with it, including all of our daily living expenses. The YUCCA system also gives us a mechanism for gathering money to use on projects that help our community develop and thrive.

The YUCCA system is a modified version of a LETS (Local Exchange Trading System), a general term for locally-initiated, democratically-organized, not-for-profit community enterprises that provide a community information service and record transactions of members exchanging goods and services. The term LETS originated in 1983 by Michael Linton. You can read the Wikipedia article about LETS for more information. YUCCA balances are tracked in a database that’s accessible through an online interface. Users can log in to see their balances, view a list of goods and services being offered, and make exchanges.

How the YUCCA System Works

Our daily interactions with the YUCCA system are very simple. It works a lot like PayPal, but without the fees. Similar to PayPal or any other bank account, YUCCAs go into your account when other people send them from their own accounts. YUCCAs leave your account when you send them to other people in the system. The more you acquire, the more you have to spend.

Organizations and businesses like accepting YUCCAs for payment because the online database keeps a record of exchanges in real time, which is an excellent way to double-check bookkeeping. Having a convenient online system also encourages people to pay promptly. People can use the local currency to pay all of their living expenses as well as many luxuries. Housing, transportation, food, telephone, laundry, photocopies, beer, coffee, showers, massages and much more are all available from YUCCA-accepting businesses and co-ops.

The key to understanding how the YUCCA system works is to start with the concept that everyone starts with zero YUCCAs.  Every time a transaction occurs between two accounts, one account goes down and the other goes up by the exact same amount.  The total number of positive YUCCAs in all accounts is always equal to the total number of negative YUCCAs in all accounts.

Currently, individuals are not permitted to have negative account balances. Some community organizations are permitted to carry negative balances, with feedback and oversight by the YUCCA-using community and YUCCAs committee.  In some ways, a negative balance allowance is like a loan or line of credit. It means that we as a community give these organizations the right to use YUCCAs before they’ve accumulated them. Unlike a loan or line of credit, though, there’s no penalty or interest for having a negative balance for any length of time. We do screen requests for negative balance allowances so that we only give them to organizations shown to be performing well financially. The only businesses allowed to hold negative balances are those which buy goods from outside the community with dollars and sell them within the community for YUCCAs. The account balances and transaction histories of all organizations and businesses that have negative balance allowances are public information for all YUCCA users to see.

What’s Sustainable about Local Currency

YUCCA is different from a bank account in that when we put money in, we are not giving it to the bank to invest as it sees fit.  Instead we are giving it to our own community to invest in infrastructure that will benefit us, our community, and the projects and causes we believe in. In other words, the more people who hold positive YUCCA balances and the higher those balances are, the more money we have to collectively invest in our community infrastructure and institutions. Another benefit of local currency in general, and YUCCA in particular, is that the convenience of using YUCCAs encourages us to shop locally, which is good for our environment and our local and internal economies.

By maintaining and using the YUCCA system we enable ourselves to fund projects that will make our demonstration village more viable and more effective in its mission to show the world one great way to live within our ecological means.
